Open a site in Stratora 2.4.0 and you see the whole location at once: 23 nodes across a mixed Hyper-V and VMware estate, the network topology, availability and alerts, the VMs and the services they run — one physical site, comprehended in about five seconds. That's the release in a sentence. Virtualization is no longer a separate tab you go check; it's part of the operational picture for the site it lives in.

## Virtualization isn't an island

Most monitoring tools treat hypervisors as their own world — a VMware console here, a Proxmox UI there, a separate dashboard you remember to open. Stratora 2.4.0 takes the opposite view: whatever a site runs, Stratora folds it in.

Discover a hypervisor through the normal add paths — network discovery, an IPAM subnet scan, or the setup wizard — and Stratora classifies it (VMware vSphere, vCenter, Proxmox VE, or Hyper-V), onboards it with the credential you already have on file, and brings its hosts, VMs, and datastores into the site it belongs to. Time-to-value is about 30 minutes, and a site that runs all three platforms simply shows all three.

- **Auto-generated dashboards** appear when you add a hypervisor and are cleaned up when you remove it — per-platform and site-level views with VMs and their run-state, hosts, datastores (including thin-provisioning headroom), and vCPU and capacity rollups.
- **A vCenter management-plane view** gives you the managed ESXi hosts, the VMs across them, and the datastores in one place — the inventory the way vCenter sees it, inside Stratora.
- **VM and host status** use the same at-a-glance indicators as everything else in Stratora, so a stopped VM reads the same way a down switch does.
- **Network topology maps** lay out cleanly and center on load, so a site's shape is legible the moment you open it.

## Reliability and security, hardened

New surface area is only worth adding if the foundation holds. Three changes in 2.4.0 make the platform you already trust a little more trustworthy:

- **Your alerts survive a monitoring blip.** If a collector or metrics source goes briefly unreachable, capacity and health alerts are now *preserved* rather than cleared — you won't lose sight of a filling datastore or a degraded array because a collector blinked.
- **Escalations show everyone.** On-call notifications now surface every intended recipient, including any that couldn't be reached — no silent gaps in who got paged.
- **Tighter credential handling** on collector configuration.
